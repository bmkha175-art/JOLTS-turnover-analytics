# Metric dictionary

One row per metric, in the order the questions use them. Pairs with `DATA_NOTES.md` for the reasoning behind each window and exclusion, and with `Jolts.sql` for the exact view that computes it.

| \# | Metric | Formula | Denominator / unit | Window | Edge cases and judgment calls |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Q1 | Median quits rate | `PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5)` of monthly quit rate, per industry | Rate as published by BLS (quits / employment x 100\) | Trailing 24 months from latest date in table | Total nonfarm and Total private excluded from any ranking, since they contain the four leaf industries. A single month is not used on its own; it's too volatile and revision-prone. |
| Q1 | Interquartile range (IQR) | P75 minus P25 of the same 24-month window | Percentage points | Trailing 24 months | Reported alongside the median because the spread, not just the level, is part of the finding. |
| Q1 | Coefficient of variation | Standard deviation / mean, same window | Unitless ratio | Trailing 24 months | Ranks industries by relative volatility, which is not always the same ranking as IQR (absolute spread). Manufacturing has a higher CV (0.14) than Leisure and hospitality (0.12) despite hospitality having by far the widest IQR (0.80 versus 0.23), because hospitality's quit rate is roughly 2.5 times higher on average, so the same absolute spread is a smaller share of its mean. |
| Q2 | Voluntary separation share | Quits rate / total separations rate x 100 | Percent of total separations | Trailing 24 months | Assumes quits and layoffs, plus the residual below, exhaust total separations; they do by construction once the residual is included. |
| Q2 | Involuntary (layoffs) separation share | Layoffs and discharges rate / total separations rate x 100 | Percent of total separations | Trailing 24 months | Same denominator as voluntary share. |
| Q2 | Residual (other separations) share | (Total separations minus quits minus layoffs) / total separations x 100 | Percent of total separations | Trailing 24 months | "Other separations" (retirements, deaths, transfers) was never pulled as its own series, so this is a computed residual, reported as a named third category rather than hidden. A negative monthly residual would be a data problem to flag; none occurred in this pull. |
| Q3 | Pre-pandemic baseline | Mean of monthly value, per industry per measure | Same unit as the underlying measure (a rate) | January 2018 to December 2019 (24 months) | Deliberately not 2015 to 2019; the earlier labor market was looser and would drag the baseline down. |
| Q3 | Current level | Mean of monthly value, per industry per measure | Same unit as the underlying measure | Trailing 24 months from latest date | Computed for all five measures (hires, openings, layoffs, quits, total separations), not just quits. |
| Q3 | Gap | Current level minus baseline | Percentage points, never percent change | Baseline vs. current, as above | Reported in points specifically to avoid a misleading percent change on a rate that's already small (for example, quits moving from 2.00 to 2.24 is a genuine 12% change but reads as a much bigger swing than a 0.24-point move actually is). |
| Q4 | Quits gap to baseline | Monthly quit rate minus the 2018 to 2019 baseline, per industry | Percentage points | Full series, January 2015 to December 2024 | Tracked monthly so the shape of the recovery, not just the endpoint, is visible. |
| Q4 | Peak | Maximum of a 3-month centered moving average of the quit rate, per industry | Same unit as quit rate | Full series | Smoothed so a single outlier month can't anchor the analysis. Ties are broken by earliest occurrence; this happened for both Government (February vs. March 2022\) and Leisure and hospitality (May 2021 vs. April 2022). |
| Q4 | Recovery ratio | (Peak minus current) / (peak minus baseline) | Unitless ratio; 1.0 \= fully returned to baseline, above 1.0 \= overshot below baseline | Two versions: trailing 12 months and trailing 24 months for "current" | Reported both ways because the verdict changes for Manufacturing (0.85 on the 24-month view, still above baseline, versus 1.03 on the 12-month view, essentially recovered). If peak is close to baseline the ratio becomes unstable; that didn't occur here but is worth watching if the series is extended. |
| Q5 | Layoff shock multiple | Peak monthly layoffs rate, March to June 2020 / mean layoffs rate, calendar 2019 | Unitless ratio | Shock window vs. 2019 baseline | Reported as a multiple of each industry's own baseline rather than the raw level, because the four industries' normal layoff levels differ by more than 4x and a raw-level comparison would overstate how unusual hospitality's spike was relative to its own norm. |
| Q5 | Recovery time | Months from the shock peak until the layoffs rate returns to within 20% of its 2019 baseline and stays there for 3 consecutive months | Months | From peak date forward | Tolerance is 20% of each industry's own 2019 baseline, not a flat percentage-point band, for the same reason as the shock multiple. A series that never satisfies the condition would be recorded as censored, not missing; none were censored in this pull. Recovery is counted in one direction only, so a layoffs rate that falls below the old normal reads as recovered, not as a failure to recover. |
| Q6 | Replacement ratio | Hires rate / total separations rate, per month per industry | Unitless ratio; near 1.0 means hiring backfills departures, well above 1.0 means net expansion | Monthly, and a summary excluding March 2020 to February 2021 | The excluded window covers hospitality's two separate collapse-and-rebound cycles; a shorter cut would keep one rebound while dropping the collapse that caused it. |
| Q6 | Median replacement ratio | `PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5)` of the monthly ratio, per industry | Unitless ratio | Excludes March 2020 to February 2021 | Median is reported instead of mean because the ratio is right-skewed: a small denominator (a quiet month for separations) sends the ratio far above its normal level, with no symmetric effect pulling it down. |
| Q7 | Pairwise correlation of quits rate | `CORR()` on month-over-month first differences of the quit rate, each pair of the four leaf industries | Correlation coefficient, \-1 to 1 | Three versions: full period, excluding March 2020 to February 2021, and a rolling 24-month window | First-differencing is mandatory, not optional. A correlation computed on raw levels would mostly measure the shared long-run trend common to all four industries (up through 2021, down after), not whether the industries actually move together month to month. First differences are computed on the uninterrupted series before the COVID exclusion is applied, so the gap doesn't turn one differenced observation into a fifteen-month jump. |
| Q8 | Openings-quits relationship | `CORR()` on month-over-month first differences of the job openings rate and the quit rate, per industry, split into a pre-2020 and a post-2021 period | Correlation coefficient, \-1 to 1 | Pre: through February 2020\. Post: from March 2021\. March 2020 to February 2021 excluded as the shock window | See methodology note below; the version actually run in the existing `Jolts.sql` file and in the Power BI dashboard uses raw levels, not first differences, and should be treated as superseded by the corrected version in `FINDINGS.md`. |

## Methodology note on Q8

Q7 established that correlation must be computed on first differences, not levels, to avoid measuring shared trend instead of real co-movement. The Q8 section of `Jolts.sql` was written to "reuse the Q7 machinery," but the query that actually runs (`pre_tightness_relationship`, `excl_covid_tightness_relationship`, `post2020_tightness_relationship`) computes `CORR(o.value, q.value)` directly on the raw monthly rates. The Power BI dashboard's openings-quits scatter charts also plot raw monthly levels. Both currently repeat the exact trap the brief calls out for Q7.

The corrected version, run separately against the same `jolts_turnover` table using first differences and the pre/post windows defined in the brief (pre: 2015-01 to 2020-02, post: 2021-03 to present, shock window excluded), gives a materially different and much weaker relationship. See `FINDINGS.md` Q8 for both sets of numbers and the corrected SQL in the appendix, which is meant to replace the level-based views in `Jolts.sql`.
