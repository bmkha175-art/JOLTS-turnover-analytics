# Recommendation memo

**To:** Marcus Rhee, SVP Sales & Client Delivery; Dana Whitfield, Chief People Officer **From:** \[your name\], Senior People Analytics Analyst **Date:** \[fill in\] **Re:** Hospitality desk expansion proposal and retention bonus program renewal

## Decisions requested

1. Does the hospitality desk expansion ($4.2M, 25 additional recruiters, three branch offices) proceed as proposed, ahead of the Q3 investment committee meeting?  
2. Should the $1.8M/year retention bonus program, launched in 2022, be renewed, resized, or ended at its upcoming renewal?

## Recommendation

Return the hospitality proposal for revision rather than approve it as written. Its core claim, that hospitality's high churn makes it the largest replacement market, does not survive the data. Resize the retention program rather than renew it at its current level; the Great Resignation premise it was funded on has expired.

## Three supporting findings

1. Hospitality does have the highest quit rate of the four verticals Brightpath staffs, close to 4.4% a month against 2.6% for the next highest. But its replacement ratio, hires divided by separations, is 1.06, within a couple of points of every other vertical. The enormous hiring volume behind the proposal is backfill, not net growth: the market isn't expanding there faster than anywhere else, people simply leave more often.  
2. Quitting has returned to, or fallen below, its 2018-2019 baseline in every vertical, and the sensitivity of quitting to labor market tightness has not increased since 2020\. Measured correctly (on month-to-month changes rather than raw levels), that sensitivity looks unchanged or slightly weaker in three of the four verticals. The retention program's founding premise doesn't hold on either the level test or this sensitivity test.  
3. The four verticals' turnover moves largely independently of each other month to month (correlations mostly between \-0.2 and \+0.4, stable whether or not the 2020 shock is included). Concentrating the full $4.2M in hospitality gives up a real diversification benefit, and hospitality carries the largest downside of any vertical: an 18x layoff shock multiple in 2020 and the most volatile quit rate of the four by a wide margin. Where retention spend continues, it should be scoped by vertical rather than applied uniformly. In professional services the majority of departures are employer-initiated, and a retention incentive doesn't address an exit the employer chose.

## What would change our mind

- A regional or metro-level re-pull showing hospitality's local churn running materially above the national rate used here. This is the weakest assumption in the analysis, and the most fixable one.  
- Gross profit per placement from Finance, showing hospitality's per-placement economics beat the other verticals despite the flat replacement ratio. A rate-only pull can't separate volume from profitability; that data could.  
- A formal regression test of the openings-quits relationship (interaction term, Chow test, autocorrelation-robust standard errors) showing a statistically significant post-2020 increase that the correlation-only version here missed.

## What we would need to be more confident

Gross profit per placement and an employment base per vertical, to convert these rates into addressable placement volume and properly price the hospitality proposal; internal HRIS or exit interview data, to test whether Brightpath's own contractor quit rate actually tracks the national JOLTS rate used as a proxy throughout; and a re-pull of the most recent one to two months of JOLTS data before final delivery, since those months are preliminary and subject to revision.

We are not proposing an alternative split of the $4.2M. That requires two inputs Brightpath hasn't supplied, gross profit per placement and an employment base per vertical, and is a different piece of work rather than an extension of this review.
